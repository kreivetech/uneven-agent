# LinkedIn Content & Visual Design

Editorial and visual standards for high-engagement organic LinkedIn posts.

## Copy & Structure Guidelines

- **Immediate 2-line hook:** Open directly with a concrete fact, operational risk, or measurable outcome before the "see more" fold. Never start with a generic institutional introduction.
- **Short and breathable (2–6 lines):** Deliver **one central idea per post**. Avoid dense walls of text or bundling multiple topics or products into a single post.
- **Decision-maker pain focus:** Prioritize practical, real-world problems of the project's target audience (operational efficiency, risk reduction, time/cost savings, or measurable gains), backed by tangible examples from the domain.
- **Hashtag hygiene:** Use **0 to 3 hashtags** maximum.
- **No external links in the post body:** Avoid direct URLs in the main text (use soft CTAs such as "send a message here" or "check the link on the profile") to protect organic reach.
- **Amplification:** When publishing from an organization page, share and engage from the personal profile to boost initial organic distribution.

## Visual Design Guidelines

- **Minimalism (2-second scan):** Keep **50% to 80% whitespace** and limit each visual to **1 or 2 main elements**. Avoid text walls, excessive logos, QR codes, or generic stock photography.
- **Color palette:** Follow the project's brand palette when defined. As a suggested default for technical or B2B content, a clean dark-mode palette (neutral dark background with a subtle grid and **1 primary accent color** for emphasis or alerts) works well—keep contrast high and avoid multi-color clutter.
- **Priority formats:**
  1. Single-concept diagrams or charts (maximum 4–6 short labels).
  2. Single-metric cards (one strong number + a 2–4 word caption).
  3. Short PDF carousels (2–5 slides) with a 1-sentence impact cover and saveable checklists.
  4. Short product clips (15–30s) starting directly in the UI action, with no long intro bumper.
